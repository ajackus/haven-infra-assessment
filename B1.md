# B1 – The Glacier Cost Gotcha

## Scenario

A seller uploads property photos on Day 0. The photos are archived to Amazon S3 Glacier after 30 days. On Day 40, the seller requests deletion.

The photos have therefore remained in Glacier storage for only 10 days.

## 1. Actual Cost Impact

Amazon S3 Glacier storage classes have minimum storage-duration requirements.

| Storage class | Minimum storage duration |
|---|---:|
| S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval | 90 days |
| S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval | 90 days |
| S3 Glacier Deep Archive | 180 days |

If an object is deleted before completing its minimum storage duration, Amazon S3 can charge for the remaining minimum-duration period.

For example, assuming S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval:

- Archive date: Day 30.
- Deletion date: Day 40.
- Actual storage duration: 10 days.
- Minimum storage duration: 90 days.
- Remaining chargeable duration: 80 days.

The seller's deletion request does not automatically eliminate this early-deletion charge.

The actual amount depends on the object's size, storage rate, storage class, region and applicable billing terms.

## 2. Recommended Lifecycle Redesign

I would redesign the lifecycle to consider both business deletion requirements and AWS storage costs.

### A. Review the storage class

Select the appropriate Glacier storage class based on retrieval requirements, retention duration and cost.

### B. Introduce deletion-aware lifecycle management

Maintain the object's upload date, archive date, landlord account ID and deletion request status.

Deletion requests should be processed through a controlled workflow rather than assuming that immediate deletion is always the most cost-effective option.

### C. Evaluate minimum-duration costs

Before deleting an archived object, calculate the remaining minimum-duration charge.

Compare the early-deletion charge with the cost of retaining the object until its minimum storage duration is complete.

### D. Implement cost monitoring

Use AWS Cost Explorer, S3 Storage Lens and appropriate billing alerts to monitor archival storage costs and early-deletion charges.

### E. Respect data-retention requirements

A deletion request must still be handled according to the application's contractual, privacy and legal requirements. Retaining data solely to avoid a storage charge must not violate an applicable deletion obligation.

## 3. Recommended Approach

I would introduce a deletion-aware archival workflow:

1. Upload property photos to S3 Standard.
2. Archive eligible sold-listing photos after 30 days.
3. Track the selected storage class and archival date.
4. When a deletion request arrives, evaluate the remaining minimum-duration charge.
5. Process deletion according to the applicable retention and deletion requirements.
6. Monitor early-deletion charges and adjust lifecycle policies based on actual usage.

## Conclusion

The key issue is that deleting an object does not necessarily eliminate its minimum storage-duration charges.

A deletion-aware lifecycle, appropriate storage-class selection, cost monitoring and compliance with data-retention requirements can prevent unexpected S3 Glacier costs.
